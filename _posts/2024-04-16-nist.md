## Finding the internship

On my 23rd birthday: November 14th, 2016, I opened Facebook (as everyday) and saw a post on the TELECOM Nancy group about an internship offer at the National Institue of Standards & Technology (NIST) in the USA. I was immediately interested. Without truly knowing why, actually.

This was not the first time I heard about NIST. A month before that, I welcomed Martial, a former TN student, on our campus. As head of the student association, I was asked to show him around our "new" building (2007) and to help him retrieve his TN hoodie. I learned that Martial was working at NIST since 1997 and that he was the first one from TELECOM Nancy (ESIAL, at the time) to go there. The following years, other students joined him for their internships, for just a few years or for their whole career and are still there now. He can be viewed as the root element that started an TN alumni network at NIST and we can be grateful to him for that! During our discussion, he explained what was NIST (I don't recall hearing about it before), what were the working conditions there (also salary), and how diverse and interesting were the research topics. I remember him mentionning how prestigious the place is in the US and bragging about his former interns now working at Google, YouTube and Twitch. I take this opportunity to highlight this event as another example of how getting involved in the associative life can help for networking.

The 3rd year at TELECOM Nancy is not the most diffuclt one, but it can revealed itself to be somewhat stressful, especially when it comes to look for the last-year internship. Although we might have a lot of options (hello Service Providers), time flies and we are pressured to accept an offer after any interview (they know that giving us time means letting us consider other options). At the time, I didn't know that I could [use maths to help me on this problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretary_problem), so I used my gut feeling. Well, I also had a somewhat of a plan: to get the most diverse experience from my internships to test and see what I enjoy the most. I already spent both of my internship in the French private sector in two small companies so this opportunity, abroad in the public sector and in a large research lab felt like a good match.

Some people say that one should choose carefully his last-year internship as it often leads to an employment and would have a significant impact on his career. I find this opinion both true and false. On one side we, software engineers, are lucky to belong to an active field of work and we are able to switch job domains quite easily if we want, on another side, I myself ended up quite impacted by my first choice as I continue working in the same domain, mostly because I realised that I wanted to capitalise on my previous experiences and not start from scratch. I know many people that continued working on the same domain as their last year internship and their major, and many that completely changed career path, to the extent of leaving the software world. Everything is possible, everything is fine, do what makes you happy.
